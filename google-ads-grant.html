Overview

What Are Google Ad Grants?

Google Ad Grants provides qualifying nonprofit organizations with $10,000 per month in complimentary Google Search advertising. This translates to up to $120,000 in yearly ad spend that nonprofits can leverage to build awareness, drive donations, find volunteers, and advance their causes through targeted search advertising.

Since its inception in 2003, the program has distributed over $10 billion in free advertising to more than 115,000 nonprofit organizations across 51 countries. It remains one of the most valuable in-kind technology programs available to the nonprofit sector.

How Do the Ads Appear?

Ad Grant campaigns show up on Google's search results page, positioned just like standard paid Search Ads. They are activated by keywords — the same mechanism used in regular Search campaigns — which means you can reach people actively searching for terms connected to your cause or services.

From the user's perspective, these ads are indistinguishable from regular Google Search Ads. The only difference? They cost your organization nothing.

Who Benefits Most?

If you're a smaller organization, you might wonder whether you can fully leverage this program. The good news: Google Ad Grants is built for nonprofits of every size. As long as people are searching for topics related to your work and you have a website to send them to, you can benefit.

In practice, the program helps level the playing field. Smaller nonprofits that might otherwise struggle to compete for visibility on Google can appear alongside much larger organizations — at no cost.

Requirements

Eligibility Requirements

To access the monthly $10,000 in free advertising, your organization must meet specific criteria established by Google.

What Does 501(c)(3) Status Mean?

In the United States, your nonprofit must hold official IRS recognition as a 501(c)(3) organization. This confirms tax-exempt status and indicates your organization operates exclusively for charitable, educational, religious, scientific, or literary purposes. To maintain this status, organizations must:

  • Be registered with the IRS as a public charity or private foundation
  • Refrain from political campaigning or substantial lobbying activities
  • Ensure no earnings benefit private individuals or shareholders
  • Direct all resources toward their stated mission

Eligible Organizations

  • Animal welfare and conservation nonprofits
  • Educational initiatives and scholarship providers
  • Environmental and sustainability organizations
  • Social justice and human rights groups
  • Health and wellness nonprofits (non-hospital)
  • Arts, culture, and community organizations

Not Eligible

  • Government entities or agencies
  • Hospitals or healthcare institutions (charitable foundations tied to hospitals may still qualify)
  • Schools, universities, or academic institutions (educational nonprofits may qualify if not degree-granting or tuition-funded)
  • Fiscally sponsored projects without independent 501(c)(3) designation

Website Requirements

Google evaluates your website to assess your organization's legitimacy and professionalism. Your site must meet these standards:

  • Secure domain (HTTPS) owned by your organization
  • Clear mission statement explaining your programs and services
  • No broken links or technical issues that hurt user experience
  • Non-commercial purpose — products or services are acceptable only if they directly support your mission
  • Mobile-responsive design that loads quickly across devices
  • Content-rich landing pages — Google requires ads to drive users to relevant pages, not just a homepage

How to Apply for Google Ad Grants

Once you've confirmed your eligibility, follow these steps to submit your application. You can handle it yourself or work with a grant management partner.

Register for a Google for Nonprofits Account

You'll need your nonprofit's registration number (EIN in the US), physical address, and contact information. Start at google.com/nonprofits.

Wait for Verification

Google partners with Goodstack (formerly Percent) to confirm your nonprofit's eligibility. This process typically takes 2 to 14 business days and may involve follow-up requests.

Submit Your Website for Review

Google will verify that your site meets quality standards — including HTTPS security, relevant content, and no broken links or technical issues.

Watch the Required Welcome Video

A short orientation video that outlines how the Ad Grant works and what's expected when managing your account.

Activate Your Google Ad Grant Account

Once the previous steps are complete, submit your activation request. Google typically responds within 2 to 5 business days.

Best practices

Managing Your Grant Effectively

Running a Google Ad Grant account takes more than launching a few ads. To fully use your monthly credit and keep your account in good standing, you need a strategic approach.

Set Clear Goals and Target Outcomes

Before writing ad copy or choosing keywords, define what you want to achieve, who you're trying to reach, and whether you have a strong landing page for that goal. Build campaigns around your audience's needs.

Choose the Right Keywords

Connect your goals with what your audience searches for. Trying to drive donations? Target phrases like "donate to [cause] online." Need volunteers? Try "volunteer opportunities near me." Be specific and mission-aligned.

Build Strong Landing Pages

Make sure the page your ads link to matches the ad's goal, loads quickly on all devices, uses HTTPS, and clearly communicates your mission. Your landing page quality directly impacts conversion rates.

Track Conversions from Day One

Conversion tracking measures whether your ads are driving real results. It's also required for compliance. Use tools like Google Tag Manager or Google Analytics to track donations, sign-ups, and other meaningful actions.

Maximize Your Monthly Budget

Most nonprofits underutilize the full $10,000 cap. Target high-volume relevant keywords, improve your ad quality scores, and write compelling copy. A well-optimized campaign spending $6,000 effectively can outperform a scattered one using the full budget.

Run A/B Tests Regularly

After your campaigns have been running for a month or two, start testing small changes: ad headlines, descriptions, landing page layouts, or bidding strategies. Let performance data guide your next moves.

Stay in good standing

Compliance Requirements

Google enforces clear rules about ad structure, keyword quality, and performance. Failing to comply can lead to account suspension or permanent loss of access.

No Single-Word Keywords

Single-word keywords are not permitted, with limited exceptions for branded terms and recognized medical conditions.

No Overly Generic Terms

Avoid vague keywords like "free videos" or "today's news." Every keyword should clearly relate to your organization's mission and audience.

Quality Score Minimum of 3

All active keywords must maintain a quality score of 3 or higher. Keywords scoring 1 or 2 should be paused or removed promptly.

Maintain 5% Click-Through Rate

Your account must sustain at least a 5% CTR each month. Falling below this threshold for two consecutive months can trigger suspension.

Implement Conversion Tracking

If you use Smart Bidding strategies, valid conversion tracking is mandatory. Track meaningful actions like donations, sign-ups, and event registrations.

Proper Ad Structure

Each ad group needs at least two active ads, every campaign needs at least two ad groups, and you must include at least two sitelink extensions.

Avoiding Suspension

Any suspension means lost impressions, clicks, and conversions. Protect your account by:

  • Setting up automated rules to pause low-quality keywords or alert you when CTR drops
  • Reviewing campaigns weekly — automation helps, but manual oversight is still essential
  • Staying current on policies — even small violations like a broken landing page can trigger issues
  • Completing the annual program survey when prompted to avoid account flags
Getting help

Should You Hire a Grant Manager?

Unless your team has someone who can dedicate 4 to 5 hours per week to managing the account, working with a professional manager is often the smartest way to protect your grant and generate results.

A qualified Google Ad Grant manager handles everything from initial application through ongoing campaign optimization. Their work typically includes:

  • Setting up your Google for Nonprofits and Ad Grant accounts
  • Researching and selecting high-performing keywords
  • Writing mission-aligned ad copy
  • Configuring conversion tracking and analytics
  • Creating and optimizing landing pages
  • Monitoring performance and adjusting strategy continuously
  • Running compliance audits and handling reactivations if needed

Benefits of Professional Management

Faster setup with fewer application errors
Expert keyword research and campaign structure
Consistent testing and improvement cycles
Ongoing compliance monitoring
Account recovery support if flagged
Landing page optimization for conversions

How to Choose the Right Manager

  • Look for a certified Google Partner — these agencies have completed Google Ads certifications and manage significant portfolios
  • Confirm Ad Grants experience — grant accounts have unique rules that standard Google Ads agencies may not be familiar with
  • Schedule a consultation — discuss your goals and make sure the agency understands your nonprofit's specific needs
  • Avoid long-term lock-in — if the agency can't deliver results, you need flexibility to make a change

What does management cost? Google Ad Grant management typically ranges from $750 to $2,000 per month depending on the agency and scope of services. Lower-cost providers may cover basic setup but offer limited support during account issues. Higher-tier agencies usually provide deeper strategy, ongoing testing, and hands-on troubleshooting.

Allow time for results. Digital marketing takes time to optimize. Give your campaigns at least 90 days to see meaningful results, as this allows sufficient time for testing, learning, and refinement.
